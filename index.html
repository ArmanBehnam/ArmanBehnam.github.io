Arman Behnam is a Founding Researcher at Quis and a Computer Science PhD at Illinois Institute of Technology, advised by Dr. Binghui Wang. His research covers Internal World Models (IWM), personalized AI, AI-human understanding, and causal explanation, representation, and reasoning in graphs and large language models. He is the first author of the breakthrough SaCRL (structure-agnostic causal representation learning framework) published at NeurIPS 2026.

With numerous papers published in top-tier venues, Arman Behnam is a recognized rising star researcher in the global AI community. His career spans significant industry-academic collaboration, including roles as AI Research Scientist at Mayo Clinic, where he published a breakthrough human memory paper at IEEE BIBM alongside world-renowned Alzheimer’s expert Dr. Ronald Petersen.

Before moving to the United States, Arman was a prominent AI/ML researcher and educator in the Middle East. At age 22, he became a lecturer at Jahad Daneshgahi Sharif, ultimately teaching over 10,000+ students and accumulating over 30,000+ followers during his time. He also served as the Founding Head of Data Science at Golrang Industrial Group, one of the Middle East's largest industrial groups with 100+ subsidiaries, where he established and led its centralized machine learning division.

Selected Research

 RealCompanion: Benchmarking Human Understanding over Longitudinal Conversations
Arman Behnam, Sunglyoung Kim, Liangwei Yang
paper

The first benchmark built from real, months-long relationships between people and an AI companion: 10 people and 27,218 messages over up to 120 days, with every label traced to the messages behind it. It shows that people rarely refer back to earlier messages, that AI cannot tell when the past matters, and that AI assumes things about people that they never revealed.

Causal Memory Policy: Making Memory Utility Identifiable by Intervening on Retrieval
Arman Behnam, Binghui Wang
paper/ code

AI memory systems judge each memory by its effect when retrieved, so a memory that is never retrieved has no measurable value. This happens for 54–67% of needed memories on standard benchmarks. CMP fills a few retrieval slots at random with known probabilities, which makes every memory's value measurable without bias, and improves separating needed from unneeded memories from 0.54 to 0.66 AUC.

Reasoning Models Are Accurate but Unsound on Identification
Arman Behnam, Binghui Wang
code

When asked whether a causal effect can be recovered from observational data, a reasoning model can give a formula for a question that has no valid answer, and no data can expose that mistake. We build CertID, which certifies every test instance with the complete ID algorithm and checks returned formulas exactly against causal models with known answers. Accuracy alone is a poor measure of soundness.
Top-k Retrieval Leaves Memory Value Undetermined
Arman Behnam
code

A memory agent can judge its memories only from its retrieval log, but that log often cannot separate one memory's contribution from another's. We prove that top-k retrieval leaves every memory's value undetermined, however many episodes are logged, and on 14 standard benchmarks no value is determined in any of 55 configurations. LEDGER reports which values the log does determine, with confidence intervals, and retrieves so that more become determined: on pooled HotpotQA, from 0.1% to 79.7% of memories.
Structure-agnostic Causal Representation Learning
Arman Behnam, Binghui Wang
40th Annual Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems; December 2026
paper / code

Causal representation learning usually requires knowing the causal structure in advance, and choosing the wrong one throws away useful information. SaCRL learns the structure and the robust representation together, with guarantees for identifying the structure and generalizing out of distribution. It recovers the true structure on synthetic benchmarks and reaches state-of-the-art accuracy on PACS, VLCS, and OfficeHome.

Measure-Theoretic Anti-Causal Representation Learning
Arman Behnam, Binghui Wang
39th Annual Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems; December 2025
paper / project / code / poster / openreview

In many tasks the label causes the observation, while most robust learning methods assume the reverse. We give a measure-theoretic framework for this anti-causal setting that learns environment-invariant features under both perfect and imperfect interventions, with guarantees for generalizing out of distribution. It outperforms state-of-the-art methods on CMNIST, RMNIST, Ball Agent, and Camelyon17.

Causal Explanation from Mild Cognitive Impairment Progression using Graph Neural Networks
Arman Behnam, Muskan Garg, Xingyi Liu, Maria Vassilaki, Jennifer St. Sauver, Ronald C. Petersen, and Sunghwan Sohn
International Conference on Bioinformatics and Biomedicine(BIBM); December 3rd, 2024
paper / code / video

    We represent patients' chronic diseases, biomarkers, and genetic data over time as a graph, and use graph neural networks to find the causal drivers of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) progression. The causal subgraph we identify points to hypertension, arrhythmia, heart failure, coronary artery disease, stroke, lipid disorders, and sex.
Graph Neural Network Causal Explanation via Neural Causal Models
Arman Behnam, Binghui Wang
18th European Conference on Computer Vision; July 2024
paper / code / video

    Most GNN explainers highlight the parts of a graph that correlate with a prediction. CXGNN, the first causal explainer for GNNs, builds a neural causal model of the graph and trains it to find the subgraph that causally explains the prediction. It recovers ground-truth explanations more accurately than both association-based and causality-inspired explainers.
